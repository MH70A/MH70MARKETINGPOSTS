# The Story of Mary Anderson: The Brains Behind the Windshield Wiper

In the early 1900s, automobiles were a new and exciting marvel. However, driving during bad weather was a hazardous and frustrating experience. When it rained or snowed, drivers had to stop every few minutes to manually wipe off the windshield, or drive with their heads stuck out the side windows to see the road.

During a cold winter visit to New York City in 1903, a clever inventor named Mary Anderson noticed this exact problem while riding a streetcar.

## An Innovation Born from Observation

Mary watched the streetcar driver struggle to see through the sleet accumulating on the glass. Every time the visibility dropped, the driver had to open the front window—letting freezing air and snow inside—or halt the vehicle completely to clear the pane by hand.

Mary thought to herself: *There must be a more efficient way to keep the glass clear without stopping.*

Upon returning to her home in Alabama, she began sketching designs for a device that could be operated from inside the vehicle.

## How Her Invention Worked

Mary Anderson's invention was surprisingly simple yet revolutionary for its time:

* Internal Lever: A handle located inside the car allowed the driver to control the mechanism comfortably from the driver's seat.
* Spring-Loaded Arm: Operating the lever swept a rubber-bladed arm across the exterior of the windshield to clear snow, rain, or sleet.
* Automatic Reset: A spring pulled the wiper back to its starting position after each stroke.

In November 1903, Mary was granted a 17-year patent for her automatic windshield cleaning device.

## Ahead of Her Time

Despite the genius of her idea, Mary faced skepticism from the automotive industry. When she attempted to sell the rights to her patent to a Canadian manufacturing firm, they rejected it, claiming the device lacked commercial value and would distract drivers.

Her patent expired in 1920 just as the automotive industry began to boom. Shortly after, windshield wipers based on her fundamental design became standard equipment on motor vehicles worldwide.

Though she never profited financially from her creation, Mary Anderson’s keen power of observation transformed automotive safety forever.
